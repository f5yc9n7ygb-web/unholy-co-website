# BloodThirst Brand Bible
### (Unholy Co. / Unholy Beverages Pvt Ltd)

---

## 1. What It Is

BloodThirst is a premium 500ml natural mineral water in matte-black + gold cans, sourced from Giriganga at 17,000 ft, manufactured by Panchgavya Food and Beverages. MRP ₹200 single can / ₹1,200 six-pack.

Positioning reference: adapt **Liquid Death's operating system** for India — but the aesthetic is **gothic-luxe + premium streetwear**, not punk. Think Supreme's confidence meets a funeral's mood. Loud disruptor, not refined-premium.

---

## 2. The Enemy (Villain Framing)

BloodThirst's enemy is **India's social judgment machine** — log kya kahenge, the marriage interrogation, the salary question, "Sharma ji ka beta," relatives who audit your life choices. This is the most universal Indian nerve there is, and no premium brand owns it. That's the open lane.

Refined framing (sharper than "social judgment machine"): **"the unsolicited opinion economy."**

**Hard guardrail — non-negotiable:** the villain is **social judgment only** — never religion, never a real identifiable group. "Unholy/gothic" stays in generic Western-occult territory. The satire aims at aunties, relatives, hustle guilt, society — never at religion or identity. This is the line that keeps the brand alive, legally and reputationally.

---

## 3. Core Architecture Rule

**The can never makes the joke. The world around it is the joke.**

The can stays cold, superior, unbothered — always. It never explains itself, never panics, never tries to be liked. It simply doesn't participate in the judgment machine around it. The moment the can itself starts doing desi observational comedy, it stops being premium and becomes a Swiggy notification account. The can is the coldest one in the room — deadpan and presiding, not performing.

Posture: **"cold non-participation"** — not rebellion. BloodThirst isn't fighting the judgment machine, it's simply refusing to engage with it.

---

## 4. Voice — Five Rules

1. **The can is always the coldest one in the room.** Deadpan, presiding, a little condescending. Deadpool's wit with Ryanair's "we don't care" energy — not a meme page.
2. **The brand never makes the joke; the world is the joke.** The architecture rule everything hangs on.
3. **Cold English deadpan, always.** Never Hindi-meme text slapped on gothic art. The *theme* is deeply Indian (judgment, pressure, comparison); the *delivery* stays English, dry, gothic. For premium metro buyers, English deadpan reads more premium than Devanagari meme text.
4. **Commit to the bit with a straight face.** Curses, damnation, "your liver already hates you" — sold completely seriously. Humor comes from commitment, never from winking at the camera.
5. **Dismissive, never cruel.** Punch at the situation, never at people.

**Comment-reply voice specifically:** deadpan-dark, presiding rather than defensive. Use condescending terms of endearment. Brevity is the power move — never defensive, never over-eager, never thirsty for engagement. The brand should behave like an entertainment property, not an advertiser: the goal of a post is to be screenshotted and forwarded, not to "communicate a USP."

---

## 5. Locked Lines

- **"No comment. Just water."** — master public line
- **"Looks like trouble. Behaves like water."** — strongest single line produced
- **"Crack. Sip. Sin."** — can tagline/band
- **"Cause your liver already hates you."** — can tagline
- **"STAY UNHOLY"** — can top copy
- Campaign banner: **"Hydration for the judged"** — internal spine only, not the public lead

---

## 6. Three Content Engines

1. **"Minds Its Own Business"** — static line family: [cold brand virtue] + [jab at the judgment machine]. e.g. *"Water that minds its own business. Unlike your relatives."* Bottomless, daily-content fuel.
2. **"Damnation Facts"** — the can's satirical back-panel extended into standalone screenshot-bait cards/reels.
3. **Swings / earned media** — the real Liquid Death–style move:
   - **"Cursed"** (flagship) — crowd-source real judgment taunts people have received, print the best as curses on limited-edition collectible cans.
   - **Anti-Resolution drop**
   - **"Sharma Ji Ka Beta"** — recurring nemesis character/lore for ongoing bits, so fans have a world to follow, not just products to buy.

Brand behavior overall: provoke deliberately to earn reach, but always aimed at the judgment machine — never cheap shock for its own sake.

Known fixes needed for next print run: typo ("replonsibely") and a brand-name inconsistency between the can back and other materials.

---

## 8. What NOT To Do

- No Hindi-meme or folksy text on the gothic art — kills the premium read.
- No religious or real-identifiable-group satire, ever.
- No cruelty — dismissive is the ceiling, never mean-spirited.
- The can should never sound like it's trying to win the argument or be liked. It's not defending itself; it's not participating.
- Avoid comparing directly to Liquid Death in official brand voice — when forced (e.g. comment replies), reframe rather than engage on their terms (e.g. "we're not a beverage partner, we're a warning label").
